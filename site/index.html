Evidence

These sentences are written directly from the measurements above — each one is produced only when the pipeline has a number to report.

    Reading the score: This detector was tested on videos it had never seen, and got 92% of them right. It is noticeably less reliable on clips that have been re-uploaded or compressed, as most social media video has been, and on kinds of manipulation it was not trained on. Treat the result as a reason to look closer, not as proof. The timeline below tells you more than the single number.

    Services & Methodology

    Multi-Signal Detection

    Spatial Analysis

    Every sampled frame is searched for faces. Each face found is cropped and scored by a vision transformer trained to separate generated faces from real ones, giving one score per frame.

    Temporal Consistency

    Frame scores are compared across the whole clip. A sustained run of consecutive flagged frames is treated differently from the same number scattered at random, which is more often noise.

    Coverage Check

    Frames with no clear face are counted. If too few frames contain one, the system reports insufficient evidence instead of guessing from the handful it did find.

    Known Limits

    Full Transparency

    Novel Generative Models

    Unseen architecture types may lower detection confidence. Unrecognized patterns are treated as unresolved rather than clean.

    Heavy Re-compression

    Social platform re-uploads erase fine-grained camera noise, which makes a clip harder to judge. This is not currently detected, so a heavily re-compressed video is scored as if it were untouched.

    Micro-Edits

    A short edit inside a long video can be averaged away by the clip score. Check the timeline bars rather than relying on the single number alone.
